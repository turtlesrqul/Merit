# Merit Brand Book

Date: 2026-06-02  
Status: Strategic brand direction, not product implementation  
Prepared from: repo docs, hidden handoff notes, current UI copy, and `MeritPitchDeck.pdf`

## Executive Summary

Merit is strongest when it behaves like a proof layer for early-career talent, not another portfolio network and not another job board. The current product already has the right philosophical center: "Proof over pedigree." That should not be changed. It is short, sharp, memorable, and commercially useful because it names the enemy clearly: hiring signals that overvalue school, resume polish, and keyword matching while undervaluing demonstrated ability.

The brand opportunity is to turn that thesis into a complete identity system:

- Students should feel that Merit gives their work institutional weight.
- Recruiters should feel that Merit reduces screening ambiguity.
- Investors should feel that Merit can become infrastructure, not a nice portfolio app.
- Designers should see a focused system with taste, restraint, and operational clarity.

The recommended brand position:

> Merit is the proof-of-ability platform where early-career talent turns real work into trusted hiring signal.

The recommended creative platform:

> Proven by work.

This keeps "Proof over pedigree" as the core belief, but gives the product a broader system for messaging, UI, decks, sales, onboarding, and recruiting workflows.

## Brand Audit

### Current Positioning

Merit currently positions itself as a proof-of-work discovery layer for students and recruiters. The product vocabulary includes "Discovery," "People," "Profile," "Add Project," "Opportunities," "Recruiter," "Passport," "Proof Over Pedigree," "Curated gallery of ambitious builders," "Proof of work, before pedigree," "Project evidence," "Profile readiness," and "Match score."

The strongest words are:

- Proof
- Passport
- Discovery
- Signal
- Match
- Builder
- Evidence

The weaker or more generic words are:

- Profile
- Portfolio
- Opportunities
- Projects
- Recruiter dashboard
- Search users, projects, skills

The brand becomes distinctive when it talks about evidence and signal. It becomes generic when it talks like a standard SaaS dashboard or job board.

### What Works

- "Proof over pedigree" is a strong core belief.
- "Passport" is a useful metaphor for portable verified identity.
- Project cards are the right atomic unit because they move the product away from resume claims.
- The deck frames a real market wedge: early-career hiring has poor signal quality.
- The product has two-sided marketplace logic: students create proof supply, recruiters pay for higher-signal access.
- The Singapore focus gives the story a concrete beachhead instead of vague global ambition.

### What Is Memorable

- The contrast between proof and pedigree.
- The idea that student work can become structured recruiting signal.
- The phrase "trusted proof" from the pitch deck.
- The product object "public passport."
- The recruiter promise: less guessing, clearer signal, better hiring.

### What Is Generic

- "Discover talent" is used by many hiring platforms.
- "Post roles" and "candidate discovery" sound like normal ATS/job board language.
- "Portfolio links" and "project showcase" risk drifting into Behance/Dribbble/GitHub territory.
- "Dashboard" is functional but not ownable.
- The warm gallery visual system can feel like a portfolio exhibition more than a hiring intelligence product.

### What Is Unique

- Cross-course proof: coding, design, business, research, startups, and documents can all be evaluated in one structure.
- Student-first supply plus recruiter-side monetization.
- Public passport plus match rationale.
- Early-career focus, especially for markets where school credentials dominate but do not fully predict ability.
- A single platform that combines profile, project proof, recruiter workflow, and explainable fit.

### What Should Never Be Changed

- Do not abandon "Proof over pedigree."
- Do not make Merit feel like a resume builder.
- Do not make Merit only for engineers.
- Do not hide the student project object behind generic profile fields.
- Do not remove the recruiter-side commercial center.
- Do not let visual polish dilute the seriousness of hiring outcomes.

## Brand Strategy

### Brand Essence

Merit turns ability into evidence.

### Mission

To help early-career talent prove what they can do through real work, and help recruiters evaluate ability with clearer, fairer, higher-signal evidence.

### Vision

A hiring market where demonstrated ability travels farther than pedigree, polish, or proximity.

### Category Definition

Proof-of-ability platform for early-career hiring.

Alternative category language:

- Proof layer for early-career talent
- Work-evidence network
- Early-career talent signal platform
- Talent passport infrastructure
- Hiring signal layer for students and recruiters

Recommended category:

> Proof-of-ability platform

It is broad enough to scale beyond portfolios, but specific enough to avoid being mistaken for a job board.

### Core Message

Merit helps students turn real projects, skills, CVs, and work artifacts into trusted proof, while helping recruiters discover and evaluate early-career talent beyond the resume.

### Brand Narrative

Early-career hiring has a signal problem.

Students are told to prove themselves, but the proof is scattered across resumes, portfolios, GitHub, design files, PDFs, school portals, LinkedIn posts, and private messages. Recruiters are told to find potential, but they are forced to screen compressed resumes, keyword matches, and institution names.

Merit organizes real student work into a public passport. Each project becomes evidence. Each skill becomes contextual. Each profile becomes more than a claim. Recruiters can review proof, understand fit, and move faster with more confidence.

Merit does not replace ambition, education, or experience. It makes ability visible.

### Strategic Enemy

Not LinkedIn. Not Handshake. Not resumes by themselves.

The real enemy is low-signal evaluation:

- Inflated resumes
- Credential bias
- Keyword screening
- Fragmented portfolios
- Closed school portals
- Unstructured links
- Hiring decisions made with too little evidence

### Brand Pillars

1. Proof

Ability should be demonstrated, not merely described.

2. Clarity

Recruiters should understand what was built, why it matters, what skills it shows, and how it maps to role fit.

3. Access

Students without elite networks should still be discoverable when their work is strong.

4. Trust

Merit must feel credible enough for hiring decisions, not merely expressive enough for self-promotion.

5. Momentum

Every profile should feel like a live record of progress, not a static resume.

## Audience Value Propositions

### Student Value Proposition

Merit gives students a credible public passport where projects, skills, CVs, and real work become proof that recruiters can actually evaluate.

Functional benefits:

- Build a public proof profile.
- Show real projects with context, artifacts, and skills.
- Make work discoverable beyond school networks.
- Track profile readiness.
- Apply to opportunities with stronger signal than a resume alone.

Emotional benefits:

- "My work counts."
- "I can be discovered for what I have built."
- "I do not need a perfect pedigree to look credible."

Student message:

> Your work is your strongest credential. Put it where recruiters can trust it.

### Recruiter Value Proposition

Merit helps recruiters discover early-career talent through structured proof, public passports, and explainable match signals instead of relying on resumes alone.

Functional benefits:

- Browse project evidence.
- Review public candidate passports.
- Post roles.
- See skills in context.
- Use match scores and rationale to prioritize candidates.

Emotional benefits:

- "I can screen faster with more confidence."
- "I can defend why a candidate is promising."
- "I can see ability before interview."

Recruiter message:

> Hire earlier with clearer evidence.

### Investor Value Proposition

Merit is building a proof-of-ability layer for early-career hiring, starting with student passports and recruiter discovery, with expansion potential into subscriptions, visibility, institutional plans, and success-based hiring revenue.

Investor message:

> Merit turns fragmented student work into hiring infrastructure.

### University / Institution Value Proposition

Merit helps institutions make student outcomes and project capability more visible to employers.

Institution message:

> Show what your students can actually do.

### Message Do's

- Say "proof," "evidence," "signal," "fit," "ability," "work," and "passport."
- Talk about recruiters making higher-confidence decisions.
- Talk about students being evaluated by what they have built.
- Use concrete objects: project card, passport, match rationale, role fit.
- Make the product feel useful before making it feel inspirational.

### Message Don'ts

- Do not overuse "portfolio."
- Do not sound like a motivational student community.
- Do not promise fully verified credentials unless verification exists.
- Do not frame Merit as anti-education. The enemy is overreliance on pedigree, not school itself.
- Do not imply AI matching is magical. Explainability is more credible.

## Voice and Tone

### Brand Voice

Clear, serious, direct, quietly ambitious.

Merit should sound like:

- A trusted hiring partner.
- A sharp product company.
- A design-literate startup.
- A founder who understands both students and recruiters.

Merit should not sound like:

- A campus club.
- A generic HR SaaS platform.
- A social network chasing engagement.
- A luxury gallery detached from hiring outcomes.

### Tone by Context

Student onboarding:

- Encouraging but specific.
- "Add the project that best proves your role intent."

Recruiter dashboard:

- Operational and confidence-building.
- "Review project evidence before shortlisting."

Investor deck:

- Market logic first, product clarity second, design taste third.

Error states:

- Plain and calm.
- "This link cannot be previewed. Open it in a new tab."

Marketing:

- Short, evidence-led, not fluffy.

## Visual Identity Foundation

### Strategic Visual Direction

Merit should move from "warm gallery of builders" toward "premium proof infrastructure."

The current warm editorial system has taste, but it risks feeling too soft for a recruiting product. The next identity should keep the humanity and warmth, but introduce more precision, contrast, and evidence-led structure.

### Design Principles

1. Evidence first

The project artifact should be the visual hero. UI decoration should never overpower proof.

2. Institutional trust

Use spacing, typography, and structure that feel credible to recruiters and investors.

3. Builder energy

Keep enough motion, artifact previews, and project variety to avoid becoming a dead HR system.

4. Cross-discipline neutrality

Do not over-index on code, design, or business. Merit must feel credible for all serious student work.

5. Fit clarity

Every recruiter-facing surface should answer: what did they build, what does it prove, and why might they fit?

### Color Direction

Recommended palette:

- Ink: #111111
- Graphite: #2B2B2B
- Paper: #F8F7F3
- Surface: #FFFFFF
- Signal Gold: #D6A72C
- Proof Blue: #2F6FED
- Evidence Green: #1E8A5A
- Alert Clay: #B85C38
- Line: #E3DED3

Rationale:

- Keep gold as a Merit equity cue, but make it a sharper signal accent, not a dominant warm wash.
- Add blue for recruiter trust and system clarity.
- Add green for validated evidence and completion.
- Use black/graphite/paper for maturity.

### Typography Direction

Recommended system:

- Display: A precise serif or high-trust editorial face for major brand moments.
- UI: Inter, Geist, or similar modern sans for dense product surfaces.
- Data/labels: A restrained mono for evidence metadata, match rationale, timestamps, and verification-like labels.

Typography rule:

- Serif should sell the category and belief.
- Sans should run the product.
- Mono should mark evidence.

### Imagery Direction

Use real artifacts:

- Screenshots of projects
- Figma boards
- GitHub repos
- Slide decks
- Research posters
- Product demos
- Business models
- Hackathon outputs
- Design systems

Avoid:

- Abstract stock students
- Generic campus photography
- Empty gradients
- Dark blurry laptops
- Founder-core mood shots

### Iconography Direction

Use line icons only where they clarify workflow. Prefer symbols for:

- Proof
- Passport
- Match
- Review
- Signal
- Role
- Artifact
- Skill
- Shortlist

Icons should be functional, not decorative.

## Future Brand Applications

### Landing Page

Purpose: Convert both students and recruiters without making either feel secondary.

Recommended structure:

1. First viewport: brand name, "Proven by work," two CTAs for students and recruiters, live product artifact in background or first frame.
2. Signal problem: resumes compress ability; student work is fragmented.
3. Student passport: show proof card, skills, CV, artifacts.
4. Recruiter discovery: show search, match rationale, evidence review.
5. Market wedge: Singapore early-career talent and recruiter segment.
6. Testimonials/traction.
7. Final CTA by audience.

Emotional experience: credible, clear, ambitious.

### Homepage / Discovery

Purpose: Show proof immediately.

Layout:

- Dense but elegant top bar.
- No generic welcome card.
- Featured proof cards with clear artifact previews.
- Filters by discipline, skill, recency, match relevance.

Hierarchy:

1. Artifact preview
2. Project title
3. What it proves
4. Candidate
5. Skills and evidence metadata

### Project Cards

Purpose: Make work inspectable in seconds.

Required fields:

- What was built
- Role / contribution
- Skills demonstrated
- Evidence artifacts
- Outcome or impact
- Recruiter relevance

Avoid making engagement stats too prominent. Views/likes/saves can make the product feel social rather than evidentiary.

### Student Profiles

Rename mental model from profile to passport wherever possible.

A strong passport should answer:

- Who is this person?
- What role are they aiming for?
- What have they built?
- Which skills are backed by evidence?
- What is the strongest proof card?
- How can a recruiter evaluate them quickly?

### Recruiter Discovery

Purpose: Reduce screening uncertainty.

Layout:

- Candidate list/table hybrid.
- Evidence preview.
- Fit rationale panel.
- Shortlist action.
- Role requirements mapped to proof.

Emotional experience: "I can make a better call faster."

### Recruiter Search

Search should support:

- Skills
- Role intent
- Project type
- Institution, if available
- Availability
- Match score
- Evidence completeness

Search results should prioritize proof strength, not profile completeness alone.

### Onboarding

Student onboarding should ask:

1. What role do you want to be discovered for?
2. What is the best work that proves it?
3. Add one proof card.
4. Add CV/contact.
5. Publish passport.

Recruiter onboarding should ask:

1. What role are you hiring for?
2. Which proof signals matter?
3. Review matched candidates.
4. Save or shortlist.

## 90-Day Brand Focus

Single design and branding focus:

> Make the Merit Passport and Proof Card feel like credible hiring objects.

This means every screen should reinforce the same idea: work becomes evidence, evidence becomes signal, signal creates opportunity.

Single product focus:

> Recruiter confidence loop.

The product must prove that recruiters can evaluate candidates faster and with higher confidence after seeing structured proof. Student growth matters, but recruiter confidence is the commercial unlock.

## Final Brand Recommendation

Keep:

- Proof over pedigree.
- Passport metaphor.
- Project-first discovery.
- Cross-discipline proof.
- Recruiter match rationale.
- Singapore market wedge.

Improve:

- Make the product less warm-gallery and more premium proof infrastructure.
- Clarify recruiter value earlier and more often.
- Turn "project" into "proof card" in brand language.
- Replace generic profile/dashboard language with evidence/signal vocabulary.
- Create a tighter visual system with stronger contrast and less beige dependency.

Remove:

- Overreliance on engagement stats.
- Generic SaaS labels where Merit has ownable language.
- Visual softness that makes hiring feel secondary.
- Any claims of trust/verification that the product cannot yet support.

The category-defining version of Merit will not win because it looks prettier than a portfolio platform. It will win if recruiters believe student work becomes easier to evaluate, compare, and trust.
